# 1. EFS Course Report

## Working Title

**Hydroperiod Change and Ecosystem-Service Trade-offs in Lomellina Rice Agroecosystems**

## Primary Research Question

How have rice-field flooding patterns changed across Lomellina, and what
do spatial observations and local and European experimental evidence
indicate about resulting trade-offs among water regulation,
greenhouse-gas regulation/disservice, rice production and
wetland-habitat availability?

## Subquestion 1 — Ecosystem Functioning

How do hydroperiod, soil conditions, shallow groundwater, irrigation
infrastructure and farm management interact to regulate:

- soil redox conditions;
- methanogenesis;
- nitrification and denitrification;
- infiltration and percolation;
- groundwater recharge;
- plant production;
- temporary aquatic habitat?

## Subquestion 2 — Historical Hydroperiod Change

How has remotely observable rice-field flooding changed spatially and
temporally across Lomellina during 2000–2021?

## Subquestion 3 — Water Regulation

Why can reductions in field-level irrigation input differ from changes
in:

- district irrigation diversion;
- groundwater recharge;
- return flows;
- peak irrigation demand?

## Subquestion 4 — GHG and Production Trade-offs

What does local and relevant European experimental evidence indicate
about the effects of WFL, DFL and safe-AWD management on:

- methane emissions;
- nitrous-oxide emissions;
- irrigation requirements;
- rice yield?

The exact operational definitions of WFL, DFL and safe AWD remain to be
verified from the experimental evidence during Phase 2.

## Subquestion 5 — Habitat Availability

How could changes in the timing, duration and spatial continuity of
surface-water availability alter temporary wetland-habitat
availability?

This question does not authorize direct inference about biodiversity,
species richness or animal abundance without biological observations.

# 2. Conditional Publication Study

## Working Title

**Does Management Placement Matter? Fixed-Composition Rice-Irrigation
Mosaics and Peak Water Demand in a Shallow-Groundwater Canal Network in
Lomellina**

## Primary Publication Research Question

Holding the proportion of operationally feasible rice-water regimes
constant, does their spatial placement and temporal synchronization
within a connected canal-groundwater system materially change peak
irrigation diversion and groundwater-recharge/return-flow dynamics
beyond observational and model uncertainty?

---

# 3. Primary Publication Endpoint

The preferred primary endpoint is:

**Peak irrigation demand**

Operational definition should preferably use:

- maximum 7-day diversion; or
- another operationally meaningful peak-period metric justified by the
  irrigation system and available data.

The endpoint definition must be finalized before hypothesis testing.

---

# 4. Secondary Publication Endpoints

Potential secondary endpoints are:

- seasonal irrigation diversion;
- timing of peak diversion;
- groundwater recharge;
- groundwater head;
- return-flow timing;
- canal-capacity exceedance.

These remain conditional on data and model capability.

---

# 5. Configuration Experiment Principle

Management composition must remain fixed between scenarios.

Example:

- 30% WFL;
- 40% DFL;
- 30% safe AWD.

Only spatial placement and/or temporal synchronization may change.

If regime proportions change, the resulting difference cannot be
interpreted solely as a configuration effect.

# 8. Research Boundary

The project is not designed to prove that a particular irrigation
strategy is universally superior.

It is designed to determine what the available observations,
experiments and validated models support.

A robust null result for the publication hypothesis is an acceptable
scientific outcome.
