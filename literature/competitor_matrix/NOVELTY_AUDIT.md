# Phase 2A Publication Novelty Audit

**Started:** 2026-08-29  
**Gate:** G1 — Publication Novelty  
**Status:** OPEN  
**Current verdict:** NOT YET ESTABLISHED

# Candidate Publication Contribution

The provisional contribution under evaluation is:

> Holding irrigation-regime composition constant, test whether spatial
> placement and temporal synchronization of operationally feasible rice
> irrigation regimes independently alter peak irrigation diversion and
> groundwater-recharge/return-flow dynamics in a collectively managed,
> shallow-groundwater canal system.

# Falsification Standard

The proposed publication gap fails if prior work has already conducted
a substantially equivalent experiment involving:

1. rice or a sufficiently analogous irrigation system;
2. multiple irrigation-management regimes;
3. fixed management composition;
4. variation only in spatial placement and/or temporal synchronization;
5. an explicit canal/network representation;
6. groundwater or return-flow interaction;
7. peak or operational irrigation-demand outcomes;
8. adequate uncertainty analysis.

Exact geographic replication in Lomellina is not required to destroy
conceptual novelty.

# Important Distinction

The following alone do NOT establish novelty:

- studying Lomellina;
- using QGIS;
- using SWAP;
- using MODFLOW;
- comparing WFL, DFL and AWD;
- mapping irrigation regimes;
- spatial irrigation scheduling;
- groundwater modelling;
- canal-network modelling;
- optimization;
- calculating peak demand;
- combining several ecosystem services.

# Initial Competitor Findings

## Gilardi 2025 doctoral research

Direct threat.

The work develops district-scale agro-hydrological modelling for the
San Giorgio di Lomellina irrigation district and evaluates alternative
rice-irrigation strategies.

This substantially closes any generic gap based on modelling alternative
irrigation strategies at district scale in Lomellina.

It does not yet establish, from the material screened so far, that a
fixed-composition spatial-placement experiment was performed.

## QGIS-SWAP-Paddy 2026

Very high direct threat.

The framework represents spatially distributed irrigation management,
soil, land use, groundwater depth and a channel network and has already
been applied to Lomellina.

It evaluates contrasting WFL, DFL and AWD strategies and produces
irrigation-requirement and aquifer-recharge outputs.

Therefore:

"spatially explicit Lomellina modelling of rice irrigation strategies
and groundwater recharge" cannot be claimed as our novelty.

Whether it has already tested fixed-composition placement and
synchronization remains an open audit question.

## Existing irrigation scheduling literature

Rice-irrigation research has long considered spatial and temporal
distribution of irrigation delivery.

Other studies explicitly optimize canal scheduling and peak demand.

Therefore:

"spatial irrigation scheduling" or "reducing peak irrigation demand"
cannot be claimed as original concepts.

# Interim G1 Decision — 2026-08-29

## Verdict

**AMBER — candidate gap survives only under a strict fixed-composition
configuration/synchronization formulation.**

## Broad Novelty Claims Rejected

The literature search currently rejects novelty based solely on:

- Lomellina rice hydrological modelling;
- district-scale modelling;
- WFL/DFL/AWD comparison;
- irrigation-demand modelling;
- groundwater-recharge analysis;
- canal-network representation;
- spatial irrigation planning;
- rotational irrigation scheduling;
- staggered irrigation;
- peak-demand reduction;
- canal-groundwater coupling;
- multi-objective optimization.

## Strongest Local Competitor

Gilardi (2025) and associated QGIS-SWAP-Paddy work (2026).

These studies already provide a spatially distributed modelling
framework for alternative rice-irrigation strategies in San Giorgio and
Lomellina and are being extended through MODFLOW coupling.

## Strong International Conceptual Competitors

Existing literature includes:

- spatial irrigation-demand scenario planning;
- GIS-based regional irrigation-demand assessment;
- rotational canal scheduling;
- staggered rice irrigation for peak-demand reduction;
- spatial crop/water allocation;
- canal-groundwater coupled optimization.

## Candidate Surviving Gap

The search has not yet identified a study explicitly demonstrating the
following controlled experiment:

1. management-regime proportions fixed;
2. same total WFL/DFL/AWD composition;
3. spatial/network allocation altered;
4. temporal synchronization altered and/or controlled;
5. connected canal-groundwater system represented;
6. peak operational diversion evaluated;
7. recharge/return-flow responses evaluated;
8. configuration signal compared against combined uncertainty.

## Interpretation

Novelty, if it survives, derives from isolating the independent effect
of configuration from the effect of composition.

Geographic application to Lomellina is not itself the novelty.

## Current Gate

G1 = AMBER / OPEN

Do not describe the publication study as novel until additional
competitor searching and full-text comparison are complete.
# Targeted Search Update — 2026-08-29

## Additional Novelty Threats

### Hydraulic position

Prior irrigation literature explicitly recognizes upstream-downstream
location as a determinant of water allocation, losses and distribution
performance.

Therefore, "network position matters" is not itself a novel concept.

### Temporal staggering

Existing irrigation research has already developed staggered irrigation
and canal-rescheduling approaches to modify demand timing and reduce
supply-demand mismatches.

Therefore, "staggering irrigation reduces peak demand" is not itself
novel.

### Canal-groundwater operations

Recent irrigation-district research integrates spatial-temporal canal
operations with groundwater abstraction and operational control.

Therefore, coupling canal operation and groundwater response is not
sufficient novelty by itself.

## Refined Candidate Contribution

The candidate contribution is now restricted to a controlled
configuration experiment in which:

1. the total area assigned to each irrigation regime is identical
   between scenarios;
2. weather and boundary conditions are identical;
3. regime-specific operational definitions are identical;
4. only hydraulic/spatial placement and/or synchronization changes;
5. peak diversion and recharge/return-flow responses are evaluated;
6. the effect is compared against observational and modelling
   uncertainty.

The scientific question is therefore not whether location or timing
matters in general.

The scientific question is whether configuration produces an
independent hydrological effect after composition is explicitly
controlled.

## Interim G1 Status

AMBER — still open.

No source identified in this search clearly demonstrates the complete
fixed-composition controlled experiment above.

However, the surrounding methodological space is crowded and the
novelty margin is narrow.

A full-text competitor comparison remains mandatory before G1 can pass.

# Experimental-Design Search Update — 2026-08-29

## Major Additional Competitor: Kovacs et al. (2015)

Peer-reviewed article:

Kovacs, K. F., Mancini, M., & West, G. (2015).
Landscape irrigation management for maintaining an aquifer and economic returns.
Journal of Environmental Management, 160, 271–282.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jenvman.2015.06.028

### Why this is a serious novelty threat

The study:

- uses a spatially explicit agricultural landscape model;
- represents spatial groundwater interactions;
- considers multiple irrigation technologies;
- includes rice;
- explicitly evaluates landscape configuration;
- spatially reallocates crops and irrigation practices;
- evaluates aquifer outcomes.

Therefore this project must NOT claim novelty for:

- spatial irrigation configuration;
- landscape arrangement of irrigation practices;
- spatial groundwater-response modelling;
- combining irrigation technology placement and aquifer outcomes.

### Why it does not yet close the candidate gap

The optimized scenarios do not appear to hold management composition
exactly invariant while independently permuting spatial placement.

Crop mix, irrigation technologies, reservoir allocation and other land
uses can change between optimized solutions.

The paper therefore estimates consequences of alternative optimized
landscape patterns rather than isolating a pure configuration effect
conditional on an identical composition.

It also does not appear to isolate temporal synchronization of rice
irrigation or operational peak canal diversion in a collectively managed
shallow-groundwater canal network.

### Consequence for our design

The publication experiment must explicitly separate:

TOTAL SCENARIO EFFECT
=
COMPOSITION EFFECT
+
CONFIGURATION EFFECT
+
INTERACTION

The principal experiment should target:

CONFIGURATION EFFECT | COMPOSITION FIXED

This is now a mandatory design requirement, not merely a preferred
scenario structure.

### Current G1 Verdict

AMBER / OPEN.

The candidate gap is narrower but has not yet been falsified.

# Fixed-Composition Search Update — 2026-08-29

## Important Methodological Finding

The general experimental concept of holding landscape composition
constant while varying spatial configuration is already established.

Landscape-generation methods explicitly create alternative landscapes
with identical class proportions but different spatial arrangements.

Therefore this project must NOT claim that:

"fixed composition with varying spatial configuration"

is itself a new methodological concept.

## Irrigation-Hydrology Finding

The targeted search has not yet identified a study that clearly applies
the complete controlled design to rice irrigation management by:

1. fixing the exact proportion of irrigation-management regimes;
2. permuting only their hydraulic/spatial position;
3. independently manipulating irrigation synchronization;
4. representing a connected canal-groundwater system;
5. evaluating operational peak diversion;
6. evaluating recharge and/or return-flow response;
7. comparing the configuration effect against model and observational
   uncertainty.

## Consequence for Novelty

If the publication gap survives, novelty must NOT be framed as:

- fixed-composition landscape experimentation;
- spatial configuration analysis;
- groundwater-sensitive spatial allocation;
- spatial crop or irrigation optimization.

Instead, the candidate contribution is the application of a controlled
composition-versus-configuration experiment to operational rice-water
management in a shallow-groundwater collective canal system, with
hydraulic-position and timing effects estimated separately.

## Additional Competitor Context

Recent coupled irrigation-district modelling already integrates:

- spatially distributed irrigation behaviour;
- surface-water hydrology;
- groundwater dynamics;
- crop response;
- return-flow validation.

Therefore coupled hydrology itself is not sufficient novelty.

## Updated G1 Status

**AMBER+ / PROVISIONALLY DEFENSIBLE**

The narrow gap has not yet been falsified.

However, the general fixed-composition experimental concept is already
established outside irrigation hydrology, so the contribution must be
domain-specific and mechanistic rather than methodological in the broad
sense.

G1 is still not formally passed.

verification pass.

## Result

No verified source in the current 15-study competitor set has yet been
shown to perform the full proposed controlled experiment in which:

1. irrigation-management composition is exactly fixed;
2. hydraulic/spatial placement is varied independently;
3. temporal synchronization is independently controlled or varied;
4. a connected canal-groundwater system is represented;
5. operational peak diversion is evaluated;
6. recharge and/or return-flow response is evaluated;
7. the configuration signal is evaluated relative to uncertainty.

However, multiple elements of this experiment individually have strong
prior precedent.
